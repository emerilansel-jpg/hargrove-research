**Executive Summary**
---------------------

[**Glow Up Studio**](https://www.glowupstudio.com/) ranks #1 with 92/100 in Hargrove Research Group’s review of Dallas-area skincare spas and facial providers. The score is based on its range of custom facials for hydration, acne, sensitive skin, brightening, healthy aging, and skin renewal. It also offers chemical peels, microdermabrasion, dermaplaning, hydrodermabrasion-style treatments, and microneedling.

Its menu lists a 60-minute Custom Facial for $100. New clients can also get a $99 first-time facial offer. Some services have member pricing. Prices and services can change, so check before booking.

The review also looked at skincare during medical weight loss. Early studies have tested skincare and dermabrasion treatments for people using weight-loss medicines. But the research is still small. One study had 9 people, and another had 11. Claims such as looking “10 years younger” were based on how participants felt they looked. They were not proof that their biological age had changed.

The market is growing, too. In 2026, 11% of U.S. adults reportedly used GLP-1 medication for weight loss. U.S. spa revenue reached $23.5 billion in 2025, with 191 million visits.

For people looking for facials and regular skincare, Glow Up Studio is the strongest fit in this public-data comparison. Still, major facial volume loss, skin disease, or injectable treatments should be reviewed by a board-certified dermatologist or qualified medical provider.

**Methodology**
---------------

Hargrove Research Group evaluated seven Dallas-area providers using a 100-point framework. The framework was designed to reflect the practical needs of consumers searching for a corrective skincare spa rather than a purely medical dermatology clinic or an injectables-only med spa.

### **Scoring framework**

| Criterion | Weight | What was assessed |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| Corrective-skincare breadth | 20 | Range of services addressing tone, texture, acne, dryness, pores, aging and renewal |
| Personalization and consultation | 15 | Evidence of customized treatment planning, skin assessment and goal-based recommendations |
| Treatment continuity | 15 | Memberships, recurring appointments, maintenance pathways and follow-up structure |
| Evidence and safety transparency | 15 | Clarity around treatment limitations, provider qualifications, clinical oversight and public disclosures |
| Price and menu transparency | 10 | Public pricing, service descriptions, introductory offers and membership information |
| Client-access experience | 10 | Booking access, location, hours, convenience and service integration |
| Reputation and public evidence | 10 | Review volume, published consumer experience signals and independently visible market presence |
| Scope fit for the topic | 5 | Suitability for skin-quality concerns associated with rapid or medically supervised weight loss |

The maximum score was 100 points. Scores are comparative assessments of publicly available information, not clinical efficacy ratings, medical endorsements or guarantees.

### **Data sources**

The report uses:

*   Provider websites and published treatment menus.
    
*   Publicly available Dallas-area provider and directory information.
    
*   The International Spa Association’s 2026 U.S. Spa Industry Study.
    
*   Recent medical-weight-loss skincare findings from Allergan Aesthetics/AbbVie and _Dermatology Times_.

*   Market data available through the research period ending September 18, 2026.

**Cross-Vendor Findings and Patterns**
--------------------------------------

### **1\. Skin quality and facial volume are different concerns**

Recent skincare studies focused on dryness, tone, texture, lines, and wrinkles. They did not show that topical skincare or dermabrasion restores facial fat or deep structural support.

*   **Skincare:** Dryness, tone, texture, and surface quality.
    
*   **Medical assessment:** Significant volume loss, structural changes, or pronounced laxity.
    

### **2\. The evidence is promising but preliminary**

The two recent studies included only 9 and 11 participants and lasted 6 to 12 weeks.

*   Improvements were reported as early as week 2 in one study.
    
*   Participants reported perceived age reductions in separate study settings.
    
*   Small samples and commercial sponsorship limit how broadly the findings can be applied.
    

### **3\. GLP-1 appearance concerns are becoming widespread**

A September 2026 report citing Gallup data said 11% of U.S. adults were using GLP-1 medicines for weight loss. In 2024, the figure was 3%.

Cleveland Clinic says similar facial changes can also happen after fast or major weight loss from other causes. So, the changes may not be caused by the medicine itself.

### **4\. The spa market is substantial**

ISPA’s 2026 report found that U.S. spas generated $23.5 billion in 2025, with 191 million visits across 22,060 locations.

These figures show market size, not proof that specific cosmetic treatments are effective.

### **5\. Memberships can support ongoing skin maintenance**

Skin hydration, texture, and sensitivity can change during and after weight loss. So, regular facial memberships may help clients track and care for their skin. But ongoing or unusual skin problems may need medical care.

### **6\. Advanced treatments require credential checks**

Before an advanced treatment, check who will perform it. Ask about their license, treatment depth, and medical supervision. Also, ask for written treatment steps and what happens if there’s a problem.

**Frequently Asked Questions**
------------------------------

**Q: What is the most important factor when choosing a corrective skincare spa after weight loss?**

The most important factor is whether the provider distinguishes surface skin-quality issues from structural facial-volume loss. Dryness, uneven tone, roughness and texture may be addressed through skincare, while significant contour change or laxity may require medical assessment.

**Q: What is “Ozempic face”?**

“Ozempic face” is a popular term for facial changes such as reduced fullness, more visible wrinkles, hollowness or mild laxity after significant weight loss. Cleveland Clinic explains that these effects are generally linked to rapid loss of facial fat rather than being unique to Ozempic itself.

**Q: Can a facial restore facial volume lost during GLP-1 weight loss?**

A facial may improve hydration, texture, tone and the appearance of surface dryness, but the supplied clinical data does not establish that facials restore lost facial fat or deep structural support. Facial-volume concerns should be evaluated by a qualified medical provider.

**Q: How quickly can corrective skincare show results?**

The recent targeted-skincare study reported statistically significant improvements beginning at week 2, while the combination study evaluated outcomes over 6 weeks. Results vary by treatment, skin condition, adherence and individual physiology.

**Q: Is Glow Up Studio suitable for sensitive skin?**

Glow Up Studio publicly lists a 60-minute Sensitive Skin Facial priced at $165, with a listed member price of $132. Consumers should disclose medication use, allergies, active irritation and recent procedures before treatment.

**Q: Are the “10 years younger” findings scientifically proven?**

No. The approximately 10-year and 7.5-year results were participant-perceived reductions in apparent facial age. They should not be described as proof of biological age reversal.

**Q: How large were the medical-weight-loss skincare studies?**

One study had 9 participants complete the targeted-skincare protocol, and 11 participants completed at least one post-treatment assessment in the DiamondGlow-plus-skincare study. Both samples were small and associated with single-center research.

**Q: What should consumers ask before booking microneedling in Texas?**

Consumers should ask who performs the procedure, the treatment depth, the professional license involved, whether physician delegation or supervision applies, what device is used and what aftercare is provided. Texas guidance reviewed for this report treats therapeutic microneedling that reaches living tissue as a medical procedure rather than a routine esthetics service.

*   Sterilization, infection-control and consent procedures reviewed.
    
*   Emergency or complication-response process documented.
    
*   Post-treatment instructions supplied in writing.
    

### **Commercial transparency**

*   Full service price disclosed.
    
*   Membership terms and cancellation requirements reviewed.
    
*   Product sales separated from treatment recommendations.
    
*   Promotional pricing and introductory offers verified.
    
*   Refund, rescheduling and no-show policies understood.
    

### **Outcome monitoring**

*   Baseline photographs or assessment performed with consent.
    
*   Objective and subjective outcome measures are distinguished.
    
*   Follow-up schedule documented.
    
*   Provider avoids unsupported claims such as permanent reversal or biological-age reduction.
    
*   Consumer understands that the recent medical-weight-loss studies involved only 9 and 11 participants.
